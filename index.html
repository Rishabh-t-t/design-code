01 Starting point

Where ConnectX started: three teams asking different energy questions

A facilities operator, a finance leader and a sustainability team can enter the same energy system with very different questions. One needs an exception that requires action. Another needs to see cost or a forecast. A third needs progress against an environmental target.

The early concepts held much of the right material. The opportunity was a dependable path from a person's request to the data, the view and the action they needed.

One dashboard for everyone would keep everything available by showing too much. Separate fixed dashboards would multiply product structures and still fail whenever a situation crossed roles.

02 The choice

Two directions: an assistant beside the dashboard, or conversation that builds it

Two directions were on the table. The first kept a conventional dashboard and added an AI assistant. It was familiar, but the interface stayed largely fixed.

The second let conversation decide which KPIs, assets, charts and twin views appeared. It adapted more deeply, yet people still needed somewhere to keep comparing and to work with the data directly.

The chosen model combined both. Conversation identifies the work. The system matches the request to a known situation. Permitted tools retrieve the data. The product assembles the relevant modules and twin view, and the workspace stays open for ordinary use.

03 Scenarios

Eight scenarios that made the adaptive workspace testable

An interface that can assemble anything is hard to specify, test or trust. Tcules and ConnectX defined eight representative situations across system status, forecasting, asset performance and operational conditions.

Each scenario named the request that starts it, the intent detected, the data required, the interface modules, the twin's behaviour and the next action available. The model could adapt, but only within those product rules.

The scenario work connected the request to everything around it. That meant the information to display, the controls to offer and what the workspace should keep in place. Each scenario gave the adaptive interface a specific job.

04 Digital twin

Building energy digital twin UX: a 2.5D view that explains the system

Early directions explored detailed 3D buildings, maps and realistic representation. Realism was tempting, so the team tested it against a harder question: does it help someone understand the energy system?

The useful questions were narrower: which assets are connected, where are they, what state are they in, and which one matters now? The chosen 2.5D direction treated the twin as an explanation layer. It could highlight, dim or bring an asset into focus depending on the scenario.

The simpler look followed from the twin's job: to explain how the energy system works.

06 How we decide

How we decide what chat controls in an AI workspace

When people need to inspect, compare and act on changing information, we decide what the conversation controls and what stays on screen. We begin with a small set of consequential scenarios.

For each scenario we define

  1. 01The request
  2. 02The data behind it
  3. 03The permitted tools
  4. 04What the screen shows
  5. 05The action
  6. 06The way back from a mistake

For each, we define the request, the data behind it, the permitted tools, what the screen shows, the action and the way back from a mistake. The AI gets room to be useful inside limits the product team sets.

That structure is what the work opened up for ConnectX. Each new question its customers bring can become one more scenario, with its own data, tools and view, so the product grows by adding situations it understands. We think it is where AI products are heading: interfaces that assemble around the question, with the product team still setting the rules.

Questions about the ConnectX AI energy product

What did Tcules design for ConnectX?

Tcules designed an AI-native building-energy workspace for facilities, finance and sustainability teams. The work covered product definition, interaction design, eight scenarios, an adaptive interaction model and a 2.5D digital twin. A request in plain words opens a view with the relevant data, modules and assets together.

Did Tcules take ConnectX beyond a prototype?

Yes. Tcules proved the product direction in a coded demo running on sample data, so ConnectX could review a request, its data and the twin as one product. We then took the demo through to a production-ready module, live since late 2025.

What does the ConnectX digital twin interface look like?

It is a simplified 2.5D view of the building's connected energy assets, chosen after early directions explored detailed 3D buildings and maps. The twin works as an explanation layer. It highlights, dims or brings an asset into focus, so people see which assets are connected, what state they are in and which one matters now.

Why did ConnectX keep a dashboard alongside the AI conversation?

People still needed somewhere to compare results and work with the data directly. Conversation identifies the work, the system matches it to a known situation and permitted tools retrieve the data. The product then assembles the relevant modules and twin view, and the workspace stays open for ordinary use.
