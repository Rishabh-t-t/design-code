01 — The idea Product definition Week 1

A bold AI idea, and a real risk
of building the wrong thing

We started with the people, not the AI. Three users share one energy system and ask very different questions: the exception to act on, the cost and the forecast, the progress against a target.

The obvious answers were traps. One dashboard for everyone shows too much, and one per role multiplies the product. So we defined the MVP around the questions themselves.

  • SUSTAINABILITY

    Sustainability team

    Progress against an environmental target

  • FINANCE

    Finance leader

    Cost or a forecast

  • FACILITIES

    Facilities operator

    An exception that requires action

The ingredients were there. The MVP’s job was to give them a structure that responds to what a person is trying to do.

02 — The riskiest call Product strategy Weeks 1–2

Chat beside the dashboard, or chat that builds it? We chose both.

The call that shaped the MVP: give people something familiar, or something new?

We weighed both, then combined them. People ask in plain words, the answer lands on the building, and the workspace stays open to keep working. Lower adoption risk for a first release, and a clearer story for investors.

The dashboard stayed, and chat became one way to build the right version of it for the work at hand.

03 — Scoping the MVP Product definition Weeks 1–2

We cut “an AI that can do anything” down to 8 scenarios

We cut “an AI that can do anything” into 8 scenarios across 4 areas. Each one is a mini-spec: the question, the data it needs, what the screen shows and the next action. Small enough to design, build and demo in weeks.

04 — Design that ships Product design Weeks 2–8

Design by subtraction: a simpler twin, pre-built for v1

We kept the twin simple on purpose: which assets are connected, what state they are in, which one matters now. Every state of the product, from the morning view to the scan and the answer, was designed before it was built.

For v1 we pre-built high-quality building twins instead of generating custom ones, so the product launched on schedule and looked finished doing it.

In v1

  • 8 scenarios across 4 areas
  • The adaptive workspace: ask, assemble, keep working
  • A library of pre-built, high-quality 2.5D building twins
  • Twins that highlight, dim and focus assets for each scenario

Left for later

  • Complex, custom-built live twins for every building
  • Detailed, realistic 3D building models
  • Map views across a whole portfolio

Good MVP design removes things on purpose. Less to build, faster to ship, easier to understand.

05 — 8 weeks to live Development Weeks 3–8

A coded demo in 4 weeks. Live 4 weeks later.

We built the demo in real code by week 4, on sample data, so the founder could see the product working before committing to integrations.

It went straight into 2 investor meetings, with a working product on screen instead of slides.

Then we took the same code to production in 4 weeks. The demo became the starting point, not a throwaway.

06 — For founders

What founders can take from the ConnectX MVP

An MVP is not a smaller version of the vision. It is the smallest product that proves the vision works, to users and to investors.

What founders can take from this

  1. 01Scope your AI into scenarios
  2. 02Build the demo in real code
  3. 03Keep the familiar, add one new thing
  4. 04Design by subtraction

ConnectX’s MVP was built to grow, not to be rebuilt. Each new question its customers bring can become 1 more scenario, with its own data, tools, view and a way back from a mistake. The product grows by adding situations it understands.

That is how we build MVPs at Tcules: a small team where design leads and engineering ships the same week, a working demo before the big build, and a first release that looks finished because what it leaves out is left out on purpose.

What the MVP delivered

  • 8 Weeks from kickoff to live

What ConnectX’s founder says

Questions founders ask about this MVP

How long did the ConnectX MVP take?

8 weeks in total. The coded demo was ready in about 4 weeks, and the production module followed 4 weeks later. It has been live since late 2025.

Who worked on it?

A team of 3 from Tcules: 1 product designer and 2 developers, working directly with the ConnectX founder. Design and engineering moved together every week, so what was designed was what shipped.

Did Tcules only design the MVP, or build it too?

Both. Tcules defined the product, designed the interaction model and the digital twin, built a coded demo, then took that demo through to the production module that is live today.

Can you build an MVP like this for a pre-seed startup?

Yes. We start with the questions your users need answered, scope them into a small set of scenarios, prove the idea in a coded demo, then ship the MVP.
